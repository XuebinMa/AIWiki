> In one sentence: text vectors are not automatically anonymous. Song and Raghunathan report word-set recovery F1 around 0.5–0.7 for evaluated sentence embeddings; vec2text reaches 92% exact recovery for specified GTR-base, 32-token inputs and iterative attacks. Different metrics, encoders, lengths, and adversary capabilities do not form one leakage rate for all vectors.

## Mechanism: what happens on my side

An encoder maps text to vectors that may preserve information useful for recovering words or sequences. An attacker can train a decoder on text–embedding pairs, or generate candidates, re-embed them with the same encoder, and iteratively correct them. Red line: a vector match or optimization convergence does not prove a unique original. Verify success against authorized originals, sensitive fields, or explicit metrics rather than my claim that I did not store text.

```mermaid
flowchart TB
    T["Private text"] --> E["Encoder"]
    E --> V["Readable or exported vectors"]
    V --> D["Decoder trained on pairs"]
    V --> I["Candidates and same-encoder iterations"]
    D --> C["Candidate words or sequences"]
    I --> C
    C --> A["Authorized comparison: word F1/exact match/sensitive fields"]
```

## Threat surface

The adversary needs target vectors; paired training, encoder knowledge, and query access affect inversion. Exposed indexes, backups, logs, or API responses can supply them. Strong 32-token results do not directly transfer to long documents, other encoders, or lower-precision vectors. Word F1 is neither recall nor name-recovery rate.

## How the defense works

Include embeddings linked to sensitive text in access, export, retention, and deletion controls; numeric representation is not a reason to downgrade them. At-rest encryption/CMK protects specified storage/key boundaries, not against authorized runtime services processing plaintext vectors. Noise, quantization, and dimensionality reduction can change inversion and retrieval utility. Without a specified DP mechanism/accounting, their evidence remains empirical.

## Buildable recipe

```text
1. Record encoder/version, text length/domain, vector precision, and same-encoder query access.
2. Restrict reads/exports for indexes, backups, logs, and APIs; trusted services bind caller/tenant identity.
3. Minimize embedded content and handle PII; audit residual detection failures and inference.
4. Run decoder and iterative attacks on synthetic or authorized texts; separately report word F1,
   exact match, sensitive fields, and intervals.
5. Report retrieval quality when changing noise/quantization/dimensions; propagate deletion to copies.
```

**Minimal testable assertion**: with fixed attack budget and conditions, recovery metrics and utility meet declared thresholds, while read/export and deletion controls pass separately. One failed attack does not establish anonymity; cosine convergence is not a text-match test.

## Real cases / research

Song and Raghunathan (CCS 2020) report word-set inversion F1 around 0.5–0.7 without ordering; vec2text (EMNLP 2023) reports 92% exact 32-token recovery in its specified setup. Pinecone and Azure document at-rest encryption, keys, and access controls; these do not certify isolation from every runtime accessor. EDPB Opinion 28/2024 addresses case-specific AI-model anonymity, not a direct ruling on all vector stores.

## Residual risk and trade-offs

Revoking access cannot recall copies already obtained. Longer texts or new encoders can change results without establishing safety; strong short-text results are not a universal inversion theorem. Text scrubbing, vector transformations, and encryption have distinct boundaries and are not interchangeable.
