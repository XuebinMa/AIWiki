> In one sentence: Without new reliable evidence, repeated corrections may leave me using the same failed assumptions; preserve facts, rejected approaches, and the next check, handling context simplification separately from code restoration.

## Symptom

I hand you an implementation that's wrong. You say, "No, use approach B." I revise it, but it still carries the original flaw. You correct me again, I revise again, and it drifts further. Three or four rounds in, you start wondering whether I understand the requirement at all.

## Why this happens

I may keep a failed assumption as a premise even after correction, but corrections do not inevitably worsen the result. New logs, minimal counterexamples, and explicit specifications can help the same session converge. The question is whether another attempt adds reliable information, not the count alone.

Old messages may be summarized, trimmed, or retrieved, rather than every error remaining verbatim in the window. Cross-session rules and memory also depend on the product. A correction does not automatically retrain the model, but can update explicit records for later use.

I should summarize rejected approaches, current evidence, and the next hypothesis. When needed, use a concise new context rather than mechanically removing all history or restoring all files.

## Consequences

- It looks like "fixing," but it's really patching repeatedly on a broken foundation.
- The context fills with failed attempts, further dragging down later quality (see [the kitchen-sink session](./kitchen-sink-session.mdx)).
- The time you spend correcting exceeds what starting over would have cost.

## What to do instead

- **Identify what this attempt learned.** List rejected hypotheses and new evidence. Reassess attempts that repeat without new information.
- **Preserve evidence when simplifying context.** Summarize the goal, constraints, failure causes, and minimal reproduction before continuing, opening another session, or clearing context.
- **Handle code rollback separately.** After opening /rewind or the double-Esc menu, check the selected code/conversation scope and protect user/concurrent work. External APIs, databases, and untracked Bash-only files are not automatically undone.
- **Store lessons explicitly.** Persist only verified conclusions that remain applicable; do not turn a guess into long-term memory.

## Example

**Before:** I repeatedly rewrite recursion without solving a stack overflow.

**After:**

```text
Me: The requirement is deep traversal; the recursive version failed at the tested depth.
    Next, validate an iterative implementation and preserve the failing case.
    Before clearing conversation, save those facts. Before restoring code, inspect the diff
    and select the exact restore scope while preserving other changes.
```

This gives the next attempt a checkable starting point; verify success against actual cases and resource limits.
